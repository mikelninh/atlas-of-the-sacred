# Founding Review Circle

## Why it exists

Atlas of the Sacred uses beauty, interaction and narrative to make archaeological knowledge accessible. Those same tools can make weak claims feel more convincing than they are.

The Founding Review Circle exists to challenge:

- factual accuracy;
- evidence classification;
- interpretive overreach;
- misleading interface inference;
- cultural flattening;
- missing local or disciplinary perspectives.

Review is not a decorative advisory board and not a request for endorsement.

## Founding disciplines

The first circle seeks four different kinds of authority:

1. **Egyptology and Giza archaeology**
2. **Construction, structural engineering or architectural history**
3. **Religion, ritual and comparative interpretation**
4. **Egyptian heritage, museums or public education**

Additional accessibility and digital-public-history review should follow.

## Review kinds

### Internal editorial review

Performed by the Atlas project team. It checks whether:

- claims have registered sources;
- evidence status is defensible;
- interpretations are separated from statements;
- explicit limits are present;
- revisions and requested changes are recorded.

It is not independent specialist endorsement.

### External specialist review

Performed by a person with relevant disciplinary, field, professional or heritage expertise outside the Atlas project team.

An external review records:

- reviewer name or anonymous label;
- review discipline;
- conflict statement;
- bounded claim or experience packet;
- decision;
- evidence assessment;
- notes and requested changes;
- attribution permission.

## Decisions

A reviewer may choose:

- `approved`
- `approved-with-notes`
- `changes-requested`
- `rejected`

Approval applies only to the reviewed version and scope. It is not a permanent endorsement of the platform.

## Conflict policy

Relevant conflicts include:

- authorship of a cited or competing interpretation;
- excavation or institutional involvement;
- employment or funding connected to the subject;
- commercial relationships;
- advocacy positions;
- close personal relationships with the project team.

A conflict does not automatically invalidate expertise. It must be disclosed so readers can interpret the review.

## Attribution policy

The Atlas may publish a reviewer’s name, biography, affiliation or quotation only with explicit permission.

Options include:

- public name and affiliation;
- public name without affiliation;
- anonymous discipline label;
- entirely private review used only for correction.

No review may be turned into a testimonial without separate permission.

## Feedback ledger

For each material point, record:

- reviewer request;
- affected claim or interface;
- accepted, partially accepted, rejected or unresolved;
- resulting change;
- rationale;
- version or commit containing the response.

Disagreement should remain visible when it reflects a genuine scholarly dispute.

## Terms

Before substantive review begins, agree:

- scope;
- expected effort;
- timing;
- attribution;
- confidentiality where needed;
- compensation, where applicable;
- how comments and corrections will be published.

The project should not assume unpaid labour, particularly from local heritage voices or early-career researchers.

## Recruitment sequence

1. Publish the review invitation and honest current status.
2. Prepare the first bounded Giza packet.
3. Build a shortlist from official institutional profiles and relevant published work.
4. Send individual invitations explaining why that person’s specific expertise is relevant.
5. Record outreach status without implying participation.
6. Begin review only after scope and terms are agreed.
7. Publish the response ledger after corrections are implemented.

## Institutional-release gate

Do not describe Giza or the Common Thread as independently reviewed until at least one relevant external specialist review has been completed and recorded.

A single reviewer cannot certify every discipline. Public language must identify exactly what was reviewed and by whom.
