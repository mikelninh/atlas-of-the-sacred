# Atlas Editorial Standard

## Governing principle

> We neither dismiss mystery nor manufacture it.

## Facts live in claims

A page, diagram or journey may only present a factual statement by referencing a claim object. Every claim must include:

- a stable ID;
- evidence status;
- one or more sources, each with a role and access date;
- a review date;
- an interpretation separated from the factual statement;
- a clear statement of what the evidence does **not** prove.

## Evidence statuses

- **Established** — strong direct or converging evidence.
- **Probable** — a well-supported interpretation that remains revisable.
- **Contested** — credible disagreement exists about method or meaning.
- **Open mystery** — evidence establishes the question, not the answer.

## Visual ethics

Every image is labelled as one of: archaeological photograph, documented reconstruction, interpretive reconstruction, cinematic visualisation, diagram, or speculative scenario.

A cinematic image must never silently function as archaeological evidence.

## Review workflow

1. Researcher drafts claim.
2. Editor checks scope and wording.
3. Source reviewer verifies support.
4. Claim receives evidence status and review date.
5. Product surfaces reuse the claim by ID.
6. Corrections update the object everywhere.
