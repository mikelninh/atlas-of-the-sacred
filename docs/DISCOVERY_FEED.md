# Dispatches from Deep Time

## Purpose

The discovery feed keeps Atlas of the Sacred intellectually alive without turning archaeological research into a breaking-news spectacle.

A dispatch is a source-backed update explaining how a publication, official record or field archive changes the current map of knowledge.

## The five questions

Every published dispatch must answer:

1. **What changed?** — the new measurement, analysis, record or synthesis.
2. **Why does it matter?** — how the larger historical or architectural picture shifts.
3. **What does the evidence support?** — a bounded list of defensible conclusions.
4. **Where do headlines overreach?** — claims that the source does not establish.
5. **What remains open?** — the next question capable of discriminating between interpretations.

## Date discipline

A dispatch records two different dates:

- `sourceYear`: when the underlying research or record was published;
- `atlasPublishedOn`: when the Atlas published its dispatch.

The interface must never describe older research as newly discovered merely because the Atlas covered it recently.

## Claim impact

Every dispatch links to one or more registered Atlas claims.

A dispatch may recommend that a claim be:

- strengthened;
- narrowed;
- reclassified;
- reviewed again;
- left unchanged.

It does not silently mutate the claim registry. Material changes still require the normal editorial review and revision process.

## Source requirements

Prefer, in order:

1. primary research;
2. official heritage or excavation records;
3. critical editions;
4. authoritative scholarly archives.

News coverage may help identify a development but should not be the sole evidentiary basis for a dispatch.

## Publication gate

A dispatch is publishable only when it has:

- a registered source;
- at least one affected claim;
- explicit evidence-supported conclusions;
- explicit overreach boundaries;
- one open question;
- a clear source publication year;
- a separate Atlas publication date;
- `editorialState: "published"` after editorial review.

The content validator checks references and required fields. Human review remains responsible for whether the interpretation is fair.

## Founding dispatches

The first edition launches with three retrospective research dispatches already represented in the Giza evidence registry:

- the reconstructed Ahramat Nile branch;
- the characterised North Face Corridor;
- the muon-detected Big Void.

These establish the format without pretending to be current breaking news.

## Future operating rhythm

A sustainable cadence is one strong dispatch per week or whenever a material discovery genuinely affects the Atlas.

Quality matters more than volume. A quiet week is better than an inflated claim.

## Reviewer questions

Before publishing, ask:

- Is the primary source represented accurately?
- Is the evidence status appropriate?
- Does the dispatch distinguish detection from interpretation?
- Could a reader mistake an open question for a confirmed conclusion?
- Does the headline promise more than the source delivers?
- Are affected Atlas claims correctly identified?
